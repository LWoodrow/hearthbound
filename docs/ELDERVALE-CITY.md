# Eldervale city design and testing

Eldervale now has twelve connected districts, ninety-six public destinations and a finite first expedition below the city. This document distinguishes the playable package from the larger campaign vision and gives the next developer and playtester a reliable starting point. Existing regional towns, residents, adventures and saves are preserved.

## Playable city

The regional gateway remains the stable location `city`, displayed as Eldervale City. Its description identifies Eldervale Waygate. It connects the existing public roads and the city’s Lantern Ward, Delvers’ Quarter and Greenwall entrances. District streets are bidirectional. Each district contains a public junction plus eight destinations connected by a perimeter circuit and several junction lanes, rather than eight disconnected markers.

The city overview charts public districts. District maps chart public places without disclosing unseen residents. A marker can be charted but unreachable from the current street. Travel still requires a real adjacent exit, all prerequisites, and no active combat, pending party check or fallen companion.

Fifteen new named residents are authored: twelve district guides, Selka at the Last Lantern, Bora in the underground refuge and Arven at the cistern. They supplement, rather than replace, the existing cast. Their species, voice, recollections and present-tense knowledge are authored. No reviewed portraits have been assigned to this new cast: names are shown instead of borrowed or mismatched faces.

Ten small district jobs change persistent flags, current observations and resident knowledge. Five additional local pastimes record visits without awarding inventory or campaign levels. These are intentionally modest community interactions, not a full economic or faction simulation.

## District directory

### Lantern Ward

Warm windows, rain-dark cobbles, small courtyards and people who recognise their neighbours. The public association is The Lamplighters' Mutual; a visit does not grant membership. Orla Wick, a Halfling neighbourhood lamplighter, is the resident guide at the district junction.

- Crooked Lantern Frontage: The familiar inn nestles between a cobbler and a narrow courtyard. Its crooked sign rocks above warm taproom windows.
- Nine Lives Cobbler: Boot lasts crowd a low window; tiny paper cats mark completed repairs.
- Hearthcrumb Bakery: Round windows steam above a courtyard smelling of spice and warm flour.
- Lamplighters' Yard: Blue glass lanterns hang over a workbench; one street lantern has a loose shutter.
- Whisperpost Office: Brass message tubes converge above rows of numbered pigeonholes; the delivery birds argue over crumbs.
- Buttonwell Court: Neighbours share washing lines, a chess table and a stone well capped against falling children.
- Little Lantern Theatre: Painted shutters frame a puppet stage; a paper dragon waits with its wings folded.
- Rainbell Footbridge: A narrow stone bridge carries little bronze bells that ring in the rain.

### Delvers' Quarter

Scarred tables, rope merchants, expedition crews and the uneasy excitement of a descent. The public association is The Surveyors' Compact; a visit does not grant membership. Hester Rook, a Human Last Lantern expedition registrar, is the resident guide at the district junction.

- The Last Lantern: A great circular tavern encloses a railed shaft. Ropes vanish into the dark while returned crews sit under mismatched expedition banners.
- Three Knots Ropeworks: Thick coils fill a long brick workshop; a demonstration line hangs over a padded pit.
- Moth & Compass Maps: Map drawers, disputed annotations and half-finished surveys occupy a crooked shop.
- Delvers' Exchange: Returned crews sort expedition accounts beneath a crowded noticeboard.
- Returners' Wall: Brass plaques mark successful returns beside blank stones reserved for the missing.
- Copper Buckle Outfitters: Canvas packs and dented shields hang over carefully arranged sample kits.
- The Forty Beds: Boot racks run beneath a hostel's windows; wet cloaks steam in a drying court.
- Surveyors' Hall: Compasses are carved above a public hall where rival surveyors compare route drawings.

### Crown Heights

Pale terraces, clipped gardens, petitioners, ceremonial guards and doors that do not open for everyone. The public association is The Civic Chancery; a visit does not grant membership. Iven Brassleaf, a Elf public petitions clerk, is the resident guide at the district junction.

- Petition Court: A roofed arcade shelters citizens waiting to speak to the public clerks.
- Palace Forecourt: Guards stand beyond patterned paving; the palace's private doors remain closed.
- Embassy Walk: Small gardens display unfamiliar banners outside separate diplomatic houses.
- High Bell Terrace: A bronze bell hangs over a terrace with a view across rooftops.
- Glass Rose Gardens: Translucent roses catch the light above carefully pruned hedges.
- Civic Record Gallery: Public city plans hang beside models of old bridges.
- The Silver Saucer: A quiet public tea court offers polished tables under pear trees.
- Terrace Watchhouse: A duty board and a clean public bench occupy the watchhouse vestibule.

### Old City

Leaning houses, reused stone, crooked alleys and older doorways beneath newer streets. The public association is The Founders' Archive; a visit does not grant membership. Dessa Underlintel, a Dwarf Old City building historian, is the resident guide at the district junction.

- Three Lintels Square: Three doorways from different centuries stand inside one weathered arch.
- Founders' Public Archive: Fragile copies rest beneath weighted glass; the originals remain locked away.
- Candlecut Cellar: A restored shop cellar contains a bricked stair and a caretaker's desk.
- The Crooked Hour: A clockmaker's lane holds clocks that disagree politely over the hour.
- Sunken Steps: Old steps end below the paving beside an iron drainage grate.
- Second Spine Books: Repaired books form crooked towers around a shop's reading stool.
- Borrowed Stone Court: Children trace games across blocks salvaged from several older buildings.
- Museum of Small Disasters: A public display collects bent weather vanes, failed charms and a famously cracked kettle.

### Grand Exchange

A hundred awnings, shouted prices, civic scales and the smell of several kitchens. The public association is The Open Scales League; a visit does not grant membership. Renn Saffron, a Dragonborn public market arbiter, is the resident guide at the district junction.

- Hall of Public Scales: A long roof protects standard weights and a civic demonstration balance.
- Hundred Awnings Arcade: Cloth roofs make a shifting patchwork over narrow market lanes.
- Seven Hammers Auction Court: Empty display plinths face a public gallery of illustrated past lots.
- Saffron Steps: Spice merchants display covered sample jars beside a worn stair.
- Threadwater Hall: Fabric bolts make a bright corridor between merchants' counters.
- Nine Kitchens Court: A shared court smells of pepper, pastry and several unfamiliar stews.
- Copperwing Dispatch: Delivery birds perch over a public board showing their usual routes.
- Coinwright Gallery: Glass-fronted cases show old city coins beside contemporary civic tokens.

### River Quays

Rope creaks, river fog, shouting crews, warehouses and taverns facing the tide. The public association is The Rope & River Fellowship; a visit does not grant membership. Neris Tideglass, a Orc public quay keeper, is the resident guide at the district junction.

- Quay Bellhouse: A small bellhouse looks across working moorings; its public bell rope is caught on a bracket.
- Bluewake Dock: Cargo crews cross planks between moored vessels and numbered quays.
- Saltglass Warehouse Front: Salt-frosted windows look into stacks of labelled freight.
- The Brass Gull: A harbour inn displays miniature ships above broad windows.
- Morning Catch Hall: A tiled hall carries the smell of saltwater and folded market awnings.
- River Customs Gallery: A public corridor explains seals, declared cargo and old river agreements.
- Oarsman's Bridge: A broad bridge spans a canal between warehouses.
- Lanternboat Yard: Hull frames stand beneath weathered sheds along the river.

### Forgeward

Hammerbeats, red furnace light, fine metalwork and guild disputes over inventions. The public association is The Rivet Guild; a visit does not grant membership. Kesh Emberwheel, a Gnome public workshop steward, is the resident guide at the district junction.

- Public Demonstration Forge: A cold demonstration furnace stands beside a loose safety shutter.
- Old Ore Lift: An iron cage hangs motionless above a locked inspection hatch.
- Rivet Guild Gallery: Members display tools and mechanisms behind low public rails.
- Blue Furnace Glassworks: Coloured glass samples glow in tall workshop windows.
- Brass Finch Foundry: A mechanical finch turns on a slow demonstration spindle.
- Cooling Canal: Warm mist rises above protected channels outside the foundries.
- Ironmongers' Row: Tools hang neatly behind counters dusted with filings.
- Hammerfall Square: An old anvil anchors a public square where apprentices eat their lunch.

### Scholars' Reach

Book bridges, observatories, eccentric public experiments and competing colleges. The public association is The Collegium of Measures; a visit does not grant membership. Maelin Quillstar, a Elf public-library demonstrator, is the resident guide at the district junction.

- Library of Returning Books: Book carts circle an airy public hall; a small indexing demonstration has become disordered.
- Collegium Public Court: Students argue beside a fountain calibrated in several incompatible units.
- The Sealed Lecture Excavation: A public viewing gallery overlooks a locked excavation beneath a lecture hall.
- Starling Observatory Terrace: A public terrace surrounds a covered telescope and a brass model of the sky.
- Everyday Charms Gallery: Public demonstrations include self-straightening picture frames and patient warming stones.
- Bookbinder's Bridge: An enclosed bridge joins two public reading courtyards.
- The Argument Garden: Benches face chalkboards where students leave objections to yesterday's claims.
- Inkwell Arcade: Stationers' windows display ink colours and elaborate nibs.

### Temple Steps

Bells, quiet gardens, charity kitchens and neighbours of many beliefs. The public association is The Open Hand Concord; a visit does not grant membership. Avel Dawnthread, a Aasimar public memorial steward, is the resident guide at the district junction.

- Court of Many Names: A low memorial wall carries loose ribbons bearing volunteered names.
- Hall of Open Hands: A public hall shelters quiet conversation and community notices.
- Seven Bells Walk: Small shrines line a steep avenue, each with its own sound and custom.
- Stillwater Cloister: A shallow reflecting pool lies between open public gardens.
- Common Pot Court: Long wooden tables stand outside a shared kitchen.
- Candlekeepers' Arcade: Rows of protected candles burn beneath coloured glass.
- Mercy House Vestibule: A clean waiting vestibule faces closed treatment rooms.
- Pilgrims' Stair: Broad stone steps offer a view toward the city roofs.

### Mourning Gardens

Yew avenues, old mausoleums, gardeners, mourners and doors beneath the memorial stones. The public association is The Quiet Keepers; a visit does not grant membership. Tovan Yew, a Goliath cemetery path keeper, is the resident guide at the district junction.

- Public Crypt Chapel: A stone chapel holds an open crypt register beside a gated stair.
- Yew Processional: A broad path passes under old yews between named memorials.
- Garden of Unclaimed Names: Simple stones stand beneath carefully tended wildflowers.
- Mourners' Bell Court: A small bell frame stands over a protected public garden.
- Keepers' Shed: Garden tools hang beside a loose public-path sign.
- Founders' Mausoleum Walk: Large sealed family monuments line a public viewing path.

### Lowwater

Canal bridges, communal washing courts, improvised repairs and fierce neighbourhood loyalties. The public association is The Floodline Union; a visit does not grant membership. Miri Reedstep, a Tiefling neighbourhood flood warden, is the resident guide at the district junction.

- Lowwater Pumping Station: A maintained public gallery overlooks a dry inspection stair and an idle historical pump.
- Floodline Court: Neighbours mend screens beneath a loose high-water warning pennant.
- Blue Basin Washhouse: Steam curls over public washing courts behind a canal-side arcade.
- Three Planks Bridge: A carefully repaired timber bridge spans a narrow canal.
- Canal Schoolyard: Chalk games cover a protected yard beside a schoolhouse.
- Roofpatch Gardens: Small public stairs climb to communal planter terraces.
- The Patient Eel: A narrow neighbourhood inn faces a working lock court.
- Little Lock Court: Water moves through a protected lock watched from the public rail.

### Greenwall

Orchards, stables, wide parks, festivals and the city wall's sheltering shadow. The public association is The Greenward Circle; a visit does not grant membership. Bram Cloverhook, a Halfling public garden steward, is the resident guide at the district junction.

- Windspinner Green: Colourful paper spinners hang over a park rehearsal ground with tangled ribbon lines.
- Northgate Promenade: A public walk follows the wall above the stables.
- Clover Coach Gardens: Horses rest behind a public stable court bordered by flowering hedges.
- Wallshadow Orchard: Old fruit trees shade a public path under the city wall.
- Copper Carp Pond: Bright fish move under lily leaves near a public garden pavilion.
- The Listening Hedges: Low hedges form a public garden path around a central seat.
- Greenward Pavilion: An open pavilion shelters festival records and gardeners' notes.
- Wallwalk Lookout: A guarded public terrace overlooks rooftops and the river valley.


## The two taverns

The Last Lantern is the famous central delvers’ tavern with a maintained shaft into the buried city. Selka Emberglass welcomes crews; Hester Rook keeps expedition records at the Delvers’ Quarter junction. This is the city’s expedition hub.

The Crooked Lantern remains a neighbourhood inn in Lantern Ward. Its public frontage is a city destination, but it does not silently enter, reset or complete The Lantern Below. The existing main campaign stays separately resumable through the regional gateway and Game library.

## First buried city expedition

The twelve-room upper survey is persistent authored geography: Rope Landing, Pilgrims’ Steps, Upper Cistern, Buried Crossing, Seal Plaza, Echo Gallery, Surveyors’ Refuge, Ward Street, Gatehouse Workshop, Municipal Gate, First-City Archive and Citadel Threshold.

Three maintained surface approaches are playable:

- Read the Last Lantern safety ledger or the independent public survey folio at Moth & Compass Maps; deliberately descend to Rope Landing.
- Read the Public Crypt Chapel register in Mourning Gardens; use the maintained memorial stair to Pilgrims’ Steps without disturbing graves.
- Read the Lowwater Pumping Station notice; enter Upper Cistern through its maintained inspection stair. The pump does not flood or drain the city.

Only visited rooms and eligible adjacent rooms appear in the underground survey. Reading a briefing records knowledge; it never moves the party. Return stairs remain physically connected.

Copy the municipal seal at Seal Plaza and report to Hester. This is an optional civic survey, not a main-story milestone. Ward Street contains a real persistent sentinel encounter. The Echo Gallery and refuge provide a noncombat route around that street. Defeat uses the existing combat rolls, blocks travel while active, commits its resolution flag and prevents respawning.

Read either the refuge return-route chart or workshop gate instructions, then deliberately turn the workshop wheel. Reading does not open the gate. After operation the gate stays open for backtracking and current descriptions reflect that state. The Citadel Threshold is a sealed boundary, not a generated next corridor.

## Maps and artwork

The new original image is `public/art/maps/eldervale-city-v1.png`, generated with the built-in image-generation tool and visually inspected. It is a detailed hand-painted city atlas rather than a collection of basic geometric symbols.

The twelve district views use fixed focused views of this same illustration, preserving visual continuity. They are not twelve independently illustrated district assets, and their fixed interactive anchors represent public chart positions rather than precise roof-by-roof geography. The underground view is a separate revealed schematic, so the city artwork cannot expose hidden rooms.

Future art work should create individually reviewed district plates and fifteen identity-specific NPC portraits. Do not regenerate maps during play, use image recognition for routes, or assign random player portraits to residents.

## Deliberate limits

This release does not implement paid shopping, lodging, healing, rentals, faction membership, NPC schedules, public transport inside the city, procedural quests, random loot, additional campaign levels or infinitely authored depths. Public establishments can be inspected and discussed, but their names do not authorize unsupported services.

Old City’s Candlecut Cellar, the college excavation and Forgeward’s ore lift are visible future entrance sites, not functional shortcuts. Deeper citadel layers are planned rather than playable. Do not describe those entrances as open, add remote travel buttons or imply a completed service.

## Continuing world building

The long-term structure is a living surface city above several buried cities and an increasingly strange citadel. Retain everyday belonging alongside danger: residents remember help, institutions disagree, public spaces provide rest and talk, and expeditions return to people rather than ending on a reward screen.

Recommended implementation order:

1. Human-test district navigation, resident conversations and all three descents. Review map label crowding and the district image crops before additional art.
2. Deepen a few districts with additional permanent residents, linked optional tasks, reviewed NPC portraits and independent evidence rather than adding another hundred shallow places.
3. Add a fully specified trading and lodging system before promising purchases, prices, repairs or healing. Declare actual costs and persistent outcomes.
4. Author the next buried-city zone as a fixed connected graph with loops, an inhabited refuge, multiple clues and persistent encounter outcomes. Connect the sealed citadel threshold only when that zone exists.
5. Open the Old City, college and Forgeward approaches as genuine connections into authored depth locations, with prerequisites and safe return routes.
6. Add an investigation that links district institutions and older municipal records while retaining the existing Hollow Road campaign’s own disclosure and completion gates.

Proposed later depth themes are the Founders’ Exchange, forgotten neighbourhood arcades, the first city’s civic halls, a ruined citadel district and deeper unfamiliar realms. These are planning themes, not current destinations or quest promises.

## Human test route

Enter The Roads of Eldervale and start at Eldervale City. Visit Lantern Ward and inspect its directory; explore the bakery, theatre and lamp yard. Fasten the loose lamp shutter and confirm the changed observation and remembered thanks.

Return to the junction and reach Delvers’ Quarter. Meet Hester and Selka, read the Last Lantern ledger, and descend deliberately. Follow the upper survey to Seal Plaza. Copy the seal and take the gallery/refuge route around the sentinel. Read the chart or workshop instructions, operate the wheel and inspect the open municipal gate. Reach the archive and verify that the deeper threshold stays sealed.

Return through the Lowwater or cemetery stair. Visit the district junction and confirm the city overview still identifies Eldervale as the regional parent. Return to Hester and report the seal. Separately test Ward Street combat, blocked travel during combat and safe repeat entry after victory.

Finally return to Eldervale City and resume the paused main adventure. Character resources and the main story’s exact saved state should not have changed merely because you toured the city.
